02 The business · how money flows

Money in,
money out, the
spread in between.

Two-thirds of revenue is net interest income — the thin difference between what a deposit costs and what a loan yields. The rest is fees from payments, capital markets, and wealth.

The premise is simple: scale through M&A, not through fintech. The cost is patience — share repurchases, frozen until capital ratios climb back. $525M of buybacks ran in FY'25; that pipe is closed for now.

08 The takeaway · what survives

A hundred cents in.
Forty-six cents back
to shareholders.

The opening said five thirds, one company. The closing says it differently: of every dollar earned, 46¢ walks out the door as dividend. The rest stays — to integrate Comerica, to rebuild capital, to wait for the buyback to come back.

Payout ratio · FY '25 46.10%
Dividends paid $1.16B
Free cash flow $3.93B

Five thirds. One company. One sixty-eight years in. Spread still earned, dividend still paid, and one bet — the bank inside the bank — just clicked shut.
