The business · Two segments

A wholesaler of small things, at scale.

GPC moves automotive parts under NAPA and industrial MRO parts through Motion. High volume, low margin — 36.8¢ of every dollar in sales is gross profit. The model is the warehouse, the truck, the counter, the rep.

The bet on patience · 70 years

Seventy raises, in a row.

GPC has increased its dividend every year since 1956. The board approved a 3.2% raise this year to $4.25 annualized — a quiet, compounding promise that has outlasted ten recessions.

The bet · One company, splitting

98 years as one.
The board drew a line.

GPC has announced plans to separate its automotive and industrial businesses into two independent public companies. Capital allocation has already pivoted: $318M in bolt-on acquisitions, $564M out in dividends, no buybacks. The split is the bet.

Spin A
NAPA Auto
North America, Europe, Australasia. The retail-and-jobber book that 90 years built.
Spin B
Motion
Industrial MRO and OEM parts. Bearings, fasteners, fluid power. Sold to factories, not drivers.

The takeaway

One company,
about to become two.

GPC's bet is a paradox: the same boring, compounding model that produced 70 consecutive raises is now being cut in half — on the theory that two focused operators are worth more than one diversified one.

Ten thousand bins still need filling. The brake pads still need to land at the counter by Tuesday. What changes is the letterhead.
