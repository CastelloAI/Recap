02 · The machine

Williams doesn’t bet on the price of gas. It charges a toll on the molecule — gathering it, processing it, moving it, storing it. Long-dated contracts. Volume in, fee out.

FOUR SEGMENTS · ONE SPINE

Transmission & Gulf carries ~38% of the revenue, anchored by Transco. The other three — Northeast G&P, West, and Gas & NGL Marketing — feed the spine.

04 · Where the dollar goes

Pipelines are capital. Most of every dollar is the cost of having put the steel in the ground a decade ago, and the people who keep it running today.

Five lines absorb roughly two-thirds of revenue. Depreciation is the largest — the slow accounting echo of the pipeline itself.

05 · The footprint

98% of the network sits inside one country. Williams is, by design, an American infrastructure story.

The Transco trunk runs Gulf-to-Northeast; gathering webs in Appalachia and the Permian feed it. The 2% over the border is rounding error in a continental machine.

You don’t grow a pipeline by accident. You bury more steel.

06 · The bet · $4.89B

Williams reinvested $4.89B in the ground in FY’25 — Transco expansions, Gulf Coast storage, bolt-on gathering. Roughly 83¢ of every operating-cash dollar went straight back into steel.

The dividend column overshoots operating cash on purpose: 93.28% payout, ~5% annual dividend growth, ~50 years of compounded steel beneath it. Buybacks play almost no role.

07 · The corridor

Three pipelines, four ledgers. The same shippers, the same Gulf Coast LNG terminals, the same Northeast demand markets. Williams is the largest by transit, not the largest by revenue.
