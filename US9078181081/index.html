The single biggest line is people. Then services and parts. Then fuel — the diesel that turns wheels and the depreciation on the steel that holds them.

What's left? Forty cents and change — the operating margin that makes the railroad a railroad.

MP 1431The bet

For a hundred and sixty-four years, the railroad has run east and west. The next move is to run coast to coast, on one timetable. Eighty-five billion dollars says it will.

06The Norfolk Southern bet

One timetable.
One network.
Coast to coast.

A pending $85 billion merger with Norfolk Southern would for the first time stitch a single Class I railroad across the entire continent — and reshape every dollar of capital structure that follows.

$31.8B Long-term debt today. Against $18.47B equity. A debt-to-equity of 1.78× — the cost of owning steel that doesn't move.

08What survives the trip

Forty cents,
two-tenths
on the dollar.

An industry-leading operating margin. Forty cents and a fraction of every revenue dollar make it through the long haul — past the diesel, past the wages, past the steel, past the depreciation — to operating profit.

The takeaway

A railroad isn't a stock. It's a hundred-and-sixty-four-year-old machine that converts pricing power into cash, and steel into distance.

The bet is that one timetable can run from Pacific to Atlantic. The floor is that ninety-five cents of every dollar still arrives by freight. Both have to be true.
