The bench · two segments

Tools on the bench.
Fasteners in the wall.

Eighty-seven cents of every dollar SWK earns comes from the things you can hold. The rest comes from the parts that hold everything else together — rivets and threaded fasteners that live inside aircraft, cars, and factory lines.

FY2025. Tools & Outdoor sells through home centers, mass merchants, and hardware stores. Engineered Fastening serves aerospace, automotive, and industrial OEMs.

A hundred cents in · what's left

Out of every dollar,
only a sliver survives.

The blue river — cost of revenue — is the steel, plastic, lithium-ion cells, and the hands that assemble them. SG&A is the storefront. The rest is interest, depreciation, and the cost of becoming a different company (restructuring & transformation).

Footprint · where the tools land

62% of the work
still ships
to American hands.

SWK's core market is the American jobsite — the contractor's truck, the home center aisle, the suburban garage. Europe is meaningful; the rest is a side of the business still being built.

The weight · $5.31B

Acquisitions built the empire.
The bill is still being paid.

$11.79B goodwill & intangibles — the price of past mergers
$240M debt paid down in FY 2025
$1.8B CAM aerospace divestiture pending — frees ~1.0–1.25 turns

The 2010 Stanley + Black & Decker deal made the company. It also made the leverage. Today the playbook is simpler: pay it down, sustain the dividend, buy back stock — in that order.

The empire pays its bills. Then it gives a little back.

The bet · capital returns

Three lines
out the door.

Free cash flow goes three places: down the debt pipe, out to shareholders, and — newly — into buying back the stock. Capex is held tight; this is a discipline year, not an expansion year.

The takeaway

It started as a bolt shop.
After 183 years,

The opening said still building. The closing reads it differently: the building is mostly done. The next chapter isn't more brands or more borrowing. It is the slow, unglamorous work of paying down what was borrowed to get here — one quarter, one fastener, one DEWALT battery at a time.
