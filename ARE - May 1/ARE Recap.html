Recap · FY '25
A life-science REIT, in eight beats

35.9M sq ft of where medicine gets made.

Alexandria is the country's preeminent pure-play laboratory landlord — 340 buildings, eight innovation clusters, one client list of pharma and biotech tenants who can't move their wet labs to the cloud.

S&P 500 life science REIT HQ Pasadena est. 1994
Beat 02 · The business

A landlord, but for wet labs — fume hoods, vibration isolation, redundant power. You can't put a benchtop in the cloud.

02·How the rent gets paid

Long leases on specialized rooms that pharmaceutical companies will not vacate easily.

Triple-net and modified-gross structures, signed by investment-grade tenants who built their pipelines inside these walls. Roughly three-quarters of rent flows through the company's flagship Megacampus ecosystems.

Three quarters of rent comes from a handful of cluster campuses; over half from tenants with public-cap or investment-grade balance sheets. The cash flow is sticky by design.

Beat 04 · Where the dollar goes

Rent comes in. Then a building depreciates, a janitor gets paid, and an interest bill arrives — in roughly that order of magnitude.

04·The cost stack

Built like a tower — floor by floor, in order of weight.

Real estate accounting is brutal: the building wears down on paper before it wears down in life. Then the operators, the bondholders, the back office — and in FY '25, a billion-and-a-half-dollar impairment charge.

Beat 06 · The bet

Twelve and a tenth years. The longest weighted-average debt term in the S&P 500 REIT index — a bet that low rates, once locked, will stay locked.

06·Capital allocation

Long debt, fixed rate, self-funded development.

Management is not buying back stock; it is buying time. Through asset sales, dividend rightsizing, and a debt stack that runs out past the decade mark, ARE is shoring up the balance sheet through the 2026 capital plan.

Buybacks paused. $1.81B of non-core dispositions completed in 2025. The dividend cut preserves about $410M a year for the forward capital plan — capital that builds buildings, not float.

08·The takeaway

35.9 million square feet of where medicine gets made — and a −$6.35 quarter to remind shareholders the building wears down too.

The hero opened with the footprint. It closes on what survives the accounting: long leases, long debt, longer ambition. The dividend cut preserves the capital plan. The impairment closes a chapter that asset sales have spent the year writing.
