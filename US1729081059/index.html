The route · How it works

The whole company is built on a small recurring fee, collected weekly, from a million customers.

Two segments. Uniform Rental & Facility Services — the bulk of revenue — covers uniforms, mats, mops, restroom supplies. First Aid & Safety handles AED kits, fire extinguishers, compliance training. The contracts are subscription-like. The cash flows are predictable.

The footprint · North America

Cintas's whole world is the continent next door.

The route truck doesn't cross oceans. Ninety-three cents of every revenue dollar comes from the United States; the rest is Canada and a sliver of Latin America. A domestic compounding machine.

The bet · Capital allocation

Cash comes in by the truckload. It also goes out with discipline.

$2.17B of operating cash flow last year. $1.76B walks out as free cash flow after capex. That money is split four ways — and the dividend, raised every year since the IPO, gets the headline.

The lane · Who else is here

Only one direct rival actually runs the route.

Aramark is the lookalike — uniforms, mats, route trucks, the same industrial customers. Comfort Systems and Illinois Tool Works live next door: building services and industrial consumables that overlap, but are not the same business.
