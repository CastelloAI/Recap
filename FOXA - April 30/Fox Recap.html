The business · two pipes in

Two ways
the money comes in.

Every dollar Fox books arrives through one of two pipes. Ads paid by brands to reach a live audience. Affiliate fees paid by cable, satellite, and virtual MVPDs to carry the networks. The pipes feed two reportable units: Cable Network Programming and Television.

The bill · where the dollar goes

Sports eats
first.

Of every dollar in operating cost, more than a third disappears into live sports rights and production. News and entertainment programming claim the next-largest slice. The rest — overhead, Tubi, and depreciation — fits inside what's left.

Sports programming & production 35%
Entertainment & news programming 22%
Selling, general & admin 10%
Digital content & Tubi 8%
Depreciation & amortization 4%

Sports is the cost and the moat. Pay the rights holders, and the bundle still has a reason to exist.

The footprint · 95% domestic

An American
company.

Fox is one of the few media businesses at this scale that is almost entirely domestic. News, sports, and entertainment, sold to the United States.

The bet · $12B buyback

Buying
itself back.

In August 2025 the board authorized a $5B increase to the repurchase program — bringing total authorization to $12B. In the first half of FY26, Fox bought back roughly 25 million shares for about $1.8B. A semi-annual dividend, raised to $0.28. A $1.0B revolver, undrawn, maturing June 2028.
