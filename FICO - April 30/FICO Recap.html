The Bet · Buybacks

FICO does not pay a dividend.
It pays itself.

No common dividend. No big M&A. Just a relentless share-shrink: $1.4B repurchased in FY25 alone, on top of years of the same. The result reads as a paradox.

01 Total debt outstanding $2.66B
02 Cash & equivalents $134.14M
03 FY25 buybacks $1.4B
04 Free cash flow generated $769.88M
05 CapEx · trivial $8.92M
Shareholders' equity −$1.75B

Negative equity, by design. Buybacks have shrunk the share count faster than retained earnings can refill the book — a deliberate posture of a cash machine that prefers fewer shares to fatter reserves. Interest coverage sits at ~6.9×.

The biggest line on FICO's risk page isn't on the balance sheet — it's the next slide.

Competition · The Frenemy

Three rivals.
One distribution
partner with knives.

Sized by market cap. The dashed coral ring is the one that breaks the pattern: a distribution partner building VantageScore to displace FICO's per-pull royalty.

TRU TransUnion $15.1B Distribution partner turned rival. Sells FICO Scores to lenders today; promotes VantageScore to displace them tomorrow. The single most consequential threat to the Scores segment.
VRSN VeriSign $25.40B A registry-monopoly analog. Roughly identical market cap on $1.66B revenue — investors benchmark FICO against it as the cleanest "mission-critical infrastructure with pricing power" comp. Revenue +6.4% YoY.
TWLO Twilio $21.23B A software-segment encroacher. Cloud-native customer engagement & decisioning offered as an alternative to FICO's pre-configured stack. $5.07B revenue, +13.7% YoY — but trades at a P/E of 627×.
