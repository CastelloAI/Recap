A dollar of revenue, cracked open.

    The remainder — about forty cents on the dollar — covers debt service, retained equity, and the dividend. The biggest line items aren't fuel. They're maintenance and depreciation on infrastructure already in the ground.

    Sixty cents of every revenue dollar comes from Texas — the Houston electric franchise plus intrastate gas. Thirty-five from the Midwest gas footprint. The rest, a rounding error.

    Here is where a regulated utility becomes a long-duration bet. Not on what it sells. On what it builds.

    Operating cash flow funds the dividend and most of the maintenance. Growth capex is funded by debt — which is why the balance sheet tilts heavy: $17.56B in total debt against $9.67B of equity, with $90M of cash on hand. The leverage is the strategy. Regulators allow the return; the company funds the wires.

    In a regulated industry, scale is the moat — and the rivals are not insurgents. They are mirrors.

    The mirrors · scale, growth, multiple

    Three near-twins, each tugging differently.

    Atmos grows revenue twelve times faster — but it's gas-only. Ameren and CMS are the closest structural mirrors: same playbook, similar size, overlapping Midwest. CenterPoint's revenue grew just 1.35% last year. The thesis isn't growth. It's the rate base.
