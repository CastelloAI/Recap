The business · 02

One product.
Every renewal.

Virtually all revenue comes from a single segment — the wholesale registration and renewal of .com and .net domains, sold through accredited registrars. Subscription-shaped. Annual price increases baked in by ICANN. Cost of revenue: 11.8%.

The bet · 05

Equity, deliberately underwater.

VeriSign carries $1.79B of senior unsecured notes against $1.3B in assets — so book equity sits at negative $2.15B. This is not distress. It's a choice. The company has bought back so many of its own shares that its balance sheet now reads like a leveraged dividend recap.

The competition · 07

Same neighborhood,
different gravity.

No one else runs the .com registry. The closest analogues are franchise data monopolies (FICO), the registrars that resell VeriSign's own service (Tucows), and the substitute that doesn't need a domain at all (Zoom).

The takeaway · 08

They don't sell domains.
They sell the answer.

Every .com resolution, anywhere on earth, eventually routes through a registry only one company is contracted to run. That contract is the moat, the margin, and the dividend. Until ICANN says otherwise, VRSN is the answer to a question the internet doesn't stop asking.
