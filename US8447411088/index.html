The thesis·NYSE LUV

Bags used to fly free. Now they don't — and that's the bet.

Fifty-four years of one plane, one class, two free bags. In 2025, Southwest broke its own creed — assigned seats, basic economy, a fee on the second bag. The story of LUV this year is the story of a brand deciding to act like the airlines it once mocked.

// I.

For half a century, Southwest sold an argument: point-to-point, no frills, free bags, fly often. In 2026, it sells a different one.

The business·Point-to-point

No hubs. No spokes. Just lines drawn between cities.

Most U.S. carriers fly you through a hub. Southwest flies you between cities — 117 destinations, 42 states, ten near-international countries, on 803 Boeing 737s. Same plane everywhere. One model.

One fleet type means one set of training, one set of parts, one schedule logic. Standardization is the moat.

The pivot·2025 transformation

The free bag, the assigned seat, the basic fare.

In 2025, three pillars came down at once. Every one had been a marketing line for thirty years. Every one is now ancillary revenue.

Plus EarlyBird, upgraded boarding, inflight buys, and a Chase co-brand card. Ancillary, finally aligned with the rest of the industry.

The takeaway·Per dollar, FY '25

Of every dollar Southwest takes in, roughly one and a half cents survive to operating margin.
