Born of a 2015 merger, with one heritage running back to 1869. The third-largest food company in North America still sells most of America's ketchup — and is about to split itself in two.

Most of America's ketchup still moves through one ledger. So does most of its macaroni, its cold cuts, and its grape jelly.

The bill · $59.7B in goodwill & intangibles

The merger left a weight on the books.

Ten years after the 2015 deal, KHC still carries roughly $59.7B in combined goodwill and brand intangibles — what was paid above book for the brands it owns. Each year, auditors test whether those values still hold up. Each year, more is written down.
