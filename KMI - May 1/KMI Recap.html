02 The business · how it earns

Four pipes. One toll booth.

KMI runs four segments. Natural gas dominates — roughly two-thirds of cash flows come from the same network that moves 40% of America's gas. About 95% of revenue is take-or-pay: shippers pay for the capacity whether or not the molecules move.

04 A hundred cents in · where they exit

One dollar in. How it leaves.

Of every revenue dollar, eighty-four cents is consumed by the network — fuel, depreciation, maintenance, overhead, and interest. Roughly sixteen survive to operating leverage.

06 The bet · $9.3B in steel

The backlog. Pointed at LNG.

KMI is internally funding a $9.3 billion project backlog — roughly 90% of it natural-gas-related — while paying out $2.6 billion in dividends and buying back stock. The bet: U.S. LNG export demand is real, durable, and structurally underbuilt.

●  The takeaway

Forty percent of America's gas moves through one network.

Of every dollar that enters, about sixteen cents walks out the other side.

Not a growth story. A toll story — with a $9.3 billion bet that the toll widens as LNG exports scale. If the molecules keep moving, KMI keeps collecting.
