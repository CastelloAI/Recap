Two segments · One harness

How $20.40B
comes in.

Two halves of a single value chain — the copper that carries the current, and the silicon that decides what to do with it.

Segment 01 · Signal & Power Solutions
Wiring assemblies, harnesses, connectors, electrical centers, hybrid HV systems.
The literal copper. The architecture under the carpet of every car Aptiv ships into.
Segment 02 · Advanced Safety & User Experience
Sensors, compute platforms, safety software, autonomous-driving stack.
The silicon and the code — the brain bolted to the harness, sold under long-term OEM supply agreements.

A dollar comes in. Eighty-one cents leave again before anything else does.

A hundred cents in · operating P&L FY '25

Where the dollar
goes.

Cost of revenue alone consumes 81 cents of every dollar Aptiv brings in. The remainder gets distributed across R&D, SG&A, depreciation, and restructuring — leaving an operating margin of 5.8%.

81¢

5¢

3¢
Selling, general & administrative.
3¢

1¢
Restructuring & separation costs — Versigent isn't free.
Add a $648M non-cash goodwill impairment and elevated tax. GAAP EPS lands at $0.64; adjusted at $1.86.

The takeaway · FY '25

A nervous system needs a body to run through. The body is being redesigned around it — by customers who'd rather wire it themselves.

Connecting the car. Disconnecting
itself.

Aptiv generated $20.40B in revenue and $1.53B in free cash flow in FY 2025 — and decided the cleanest path to value was to take the harness business out of the company that sells the brain. The bill for fifteen years of bundling now reads as one $648M non-cash impairment, one $1.5B buyback, and one new ticker called Versigent.
