02The business · How it earns

Behind every center, a rent roll.

Kimco is a landlord. $1.36B of revenue last filing year, drawn from tenant rent and reimbursements for taxes, insurance, and parking-lot upkeep. The tenant mix is the strategy: necessity-driven, high-frequency, recession-textured.

04The dollar · Where it goes

Of every dollar of rent that walks in, sixty-nine cents leaves before it gets to the bottom line.

Real estate is heavy. Every dollar of rent is taxed by the buildings that depreciate, the loans that accrue, the towns that levy property tax, and the parking lots that need plowing.

07The bet · Capital allocation

Recycle the old, fund the new.

Kimco's playbook is disciplined accretive recycling: monetize the parts of the portfolio that have appreciated — ground leases, development entitlements — and redeploy into acquisitions that fit the grocery-anchored thesis.

09The takeaway

565 parking lots.
$1.36B walks in.
$140M walks out free.

Per dollar of rent, sixty-nine cents is paid to the buildings, the loans, the towns, and the staff. Of what's left, the dividend takes most. $140M survives — to fund the next acquisition, or the buyback that hasn't happened yet.

That's the trade. Boring rents on necessity retail, leveraged 0.74-to-1 against equity, recycled into the next center. A landlord's arithmetic.
